Episode 3

English Translation

"Gah... I'm dying...! I'm gonna die!"

Collapsed in the dorm entryway again, I screamed. Nike... I still didn't know if that was his first name or his last... and Yukiko-san and Natsuko-san came to meet me, still in the clothes they'd gone out in.

"Busy little bee, aren't you. Money always works itself out somehow."

"A job... I need a job, right now, day labor, anything...! I don't have the money to live tomorrow!"

"I'll treat you to meals, at least, all right? I cook for two anyway; three is no different."

Natsuko-san's kindness hit me right in the heart. But I couldn't just take it for free. If my old man found out, he'd fly out the same day and drag me straight back home.

"Once I find a job, I'll pay you back for the food..."

Still half-collapsed, I shifted into a full dogeza bow as I said my thanks.

"Um... you're showing me around, so I'll pay the train fare. Let's go see the campus, okay?"

With that and a smile, Yuki-chan closed the door.

Room 201, assigned to me: a six-tatami Japanese-style room. Every bit as shabby as the outside, but its faint old smell was like home, and it was oddly calming. Believe it or not: shared toilet, no bath. There's a public bathhouse right next door, included in the rent. Unlimited baths honestly made me happy, but as Tokyo housing goes, this place was pretty much unheard of.

Even if I made friends, I could never bring them here. For one thing, voices would carry like crazy. For another, they'd make fun of me.

But that was fine.

If they found out Yuki-chan lived here, they'd come up with one excuse after another to barge in.

...Feeling smug over friends I don't even have yet. Pathetic.

But what was I going to do?

The panic from before had settled, for now. But the fact remained right in front of me: I had no money.

What I'd said was no lie. If I didn't start a job tomorrow, I'd seriously be thrown out.

"A job... that's right. Earlier, I..."

I'd made it back to the dorm almost on autopilot, but apparently I'd still managed to grab a job magazine at the convenience store at the bottom of the hill. I had no memory of walking through its automatic doors, but the magazine was here, so I must have.

I was the eldest son of a hot spring inn, after all. Helping out, I was used to serving customers. I didn't know how busy places around here got, but it couldn't be worse than a tourist spot in high season.

Hmm, let's see. Convenience stores, a pachinko parlor, cafés... A café would be nice. Kind of stylish, you know?

Oh, Tokyo has arcades too. A bit farther and there were cram schools... Tutoring wouldn't be bad either.

Ah, being able to choose my own job was the best. I was glad I'd run away from home.

"That's a creepy face you're making."

"Whooooa!?"

At some point the door had opened, and old man Nike was peering in.

"Old man! Is there no privacy in this place!?"

"Hey, I knocked. No answer, so I got worried you'd collapsed again, that's all."

"That's—! ...Th-that's... thank you very much."

The old guy laughed dryly and—

"Well, well. Surprisingly honest, aren't you. Was all that before just bluffing?"

"I wasn't raised to throw kindness back in someone's face."

"Raised well, huh. Oh, right—hey, you're taking a bath, aren't you?"

"Huh? Yeah, well. I'm drenched in sweat."

"It's free for us, but there's a time limit. Eight to ten at night. Head over soon or you'll miss it."

I looked at the clock—it was already past eight-thirty.

I looked outside—the sun had completely set at some point.

"...Huh? It was bright out just a little while ago..."

"Aaah... day one in Tokyo, and it's this..."

Soaking in the bathhouse tub, I lamented for a while. The place seemed a little run-down; besides me and old man Nike, there was only one parent-and-kid pair and a few old men.

Well, quiet wasn't bad. And above all, it was spacious. Modest next to our inn's famous big bath, sure, but for daily use, this size was just right.

"Not bad, right? You left on a journey and found a roof the very same day."

"...Old man, do you actually have experience being homeless?"

"Well. Not far off the mark."

Seriously?

"Japan... no, this world is pretty blessed, you know. That's why I laze around here, I guess."

"...Were you born abroad, by any chance?"

"Not far off. In a word, I'm from somewhere more dangerous than here."

'Here' meant Japan, right? So... a war zone or something?

He didn't seem to want to talk about it, so I decided not to dig.

"But where you're from has nothing to do with my failure."

"Just listen, all right... And there's something I should tell you in advance."

"What is it? You could've told me earlier."

"Some things can't be said at the dorm... Kid, stay away from Yuki-chan."

"Pfft!"

I freaked out so hard my body sank right under the water.

The old guy hauled me up before I drowned and went on, his face utterly serious.

"Listen, I'm saying this for your own good."

"What do you mean? Is there something wrong with her personality?"

"Did she look that way to you?"

I shook my head.

"Right. She's a good kid."

"Then what is it? Playing the father—'I'll not give my daughter to some nobody'? Besides, I only just met her."

"If you're not interested in her, fine. But that girl... well, you'll see soon enough... she's precarious."

His words reminded me of what she'd said earlier.

She'd been stopped from going to the university alone, hadn't she.

Was she catastrophically bad with directions? Not that it had anything to do with me.

"The father thing—well, not far off. If anything happened to her, I'd have a lot of people to answer to. She overcame all kinds of entanglements to be at that dorm. If even one of them decided to act, she'd vanish the same day."

"...That would be bad."

"And if you make a move on her, a fair number of them will decide to act."

"A complicated family?"

"A family common folk can't even imagine."

So she was a young lady from a fine family, was that it? Then why live in a dump of a dorm like that? Even to give her life experience, a girl living alone these days would normally be in an apartment with an auto-lock.

Could I take the old guy's words at face value?

"And about the money."

"You'll lend me some?"

"I told you, I'm flat broke... but work does come in, now and then."

And the old guy looked at me, more serious than before.

"When you've got no other options left, I'll let you help with my work. You'll be paid, of course. Got it? When jobs, loans, all of it falls through, remember that."

"...Old man."

"Quit calling me 'old man.'"

"Why are you being so good to me?"

"And quit the polite speech... You're a new resident, hard won. If fate's tied a thread, I'll keep it from snapping too easily."

"...Old man!"

"I said quit calling me that!"

Warmed in body and soul, I went back to the dorm. Thanks to the old guy, I felt like I could try again tomorrow.

A good old guy. Though on the way back, the residents we passed seemed to be avoiding him.

"Oh, Taro-san. Back from the bath?"

Yuki-chan was in the entryway.

"I just got back. Free access to that bath—talk about lucky."

"Hehe. I'm glad you liked it."

In her hands were a bath bucket and a towel. She was about to head to the bathhouse herself, by the look of it.

"I'm waiting for Natsuko-san to get ready."

Hmm... Yuki-chan and Natsuko-san, in the bath... No, no, don't think rude thoughts. Gross.

"You seem... to have recovered. I'm glad. You were so down earlier."

"Well, I was down... but old man Ni—Nike-san told me that if it comes to it, he'll let me help with his work, so—"

Clatter—the bath bucket hit the floor.

"To think there's still this much kindness in the Reiwa era... Huh, what's wrong?"

"Taro-san! No! You mustn't!"

She leaned in close, and even grabbed the collar of my shirt... Whoa, whoa, whoa! Having her that close caused all kinds of problems!

"You have to refuse, no matter what! Promise me!"

"H-he said it was only if I absolutely couldn't find a job..."

"If it comes to doing Nike-san's work, I'll lend you the money myself! Please... just not Nike-san's work..."

"Um... what kind of work does he do, anyway?"

"I can't be the one to tell you... just—that it's very dangerous."

Don't tell me the old guy really is yakuza.

His 'work' wouldn't be raiding some place, or charging in as a hitman screaming 'I'll take you down!' in broad daylight... would it?

"Please... I don't want you to die."

Oh no. That settles it.

"O-okay... Um, then maybe I should keep my distance from the old guy as much as I can."

"Huh? Why? He's a good person. As long as you don't do his work, he's just an ordinary old man."

Something didn't add up! Isn't 'don't get involved with organized crime' the rule of modern society!?

Before long Natsuko-san came out, and Yuki-chan headed off to the bathhouse.

...Come to think of it, why did the residents here know about me? They said the landlady told them, but would anyone spill private details that easily?

And besides... I had a feeling there were other things that felt off...

No—first, a job.

Tonight, I'd go through the job magazine from cover to cover.

Come on, finding a part-time job would be easy. I'd land one in no time.

And then, I'd go see the campus with Yuki-chan.
