Episode 4 No Job, No Hope?

English (surviving original)

The next morning, I found myself sprawled out in the dorm hallway again, right under the communal telephone.

“Taro-san!”

O-Yuki-chan’s voice rang out as she hurried over. Her expression was filled with concern—a sight I was getting way too used to.

“Are you alright? What happened?”

“Just... just let me die here,” I groaned, barely lifting my arm in defeat.

“You can’t!” She crouched beside me, her snow-white brows furrowing. “Did something happen with your job search?”

“Oh, you mean the job search where every single place I called rejected me the moment I mentioned where I live? That job search?”

She blinked. “Wait... what?”

I sighed and sat up, rubbing my face. “Yeah. Every single place I called asked for my address, and the moment I said ‘Sakura Saku Dorm,’ it was game over. Some made an excuse about positions being filled, but others straight-up hung up on me.”

O-Yuki-chan looked genuinely confused. “Why would that matter?”

I hesitated for a second, then decided to just ask. “Hey... yesterday, when I was walking up the hill, I kept getting weird looks from people. And now this. Do you think it’s because of Nike-san?”

“Because of Nike-san?” she echoed, tilting her head.

“Yeah, you know...” I hesitated again, then muttered under my breath, “Because he’s a yakuza...”

“Yakuza?” she repeated, puzzled.

I blinked.

Wait. She didn’t know what a yakuza was?

A realization hit me like a brick. No wonder she was so naive about things. No wonder she didn’t react normally to my suspicions about Nike. She was that sheltered.

“Ah,” I nodded to myself. “That makes sense.”

“What makes sense?” she asked, tilting her head even more.

“Nothing, nothing.” I waved it off. There was no point in trying to explain the concept of organized crime to someone who probably thought “gangsters” were just something in movies.

Instead, she suddenly brightened up. “Oh! Then how about we go visit the university today?”

“Huh?”

“A change of pace! If your job hunt isn’t working out right now, it’ll just stress you out to keep pushing. Come on, let’s go look around campus!”

“I mean...” I rubbed the back of my neck. “I don’t even have train fare right now.”

She puffed up her cheeks. “Didn’t I say so yesterday? You’re showing me around, so I’ll pay.”

Before I could argue, she had already turned and started heading back to her room to get ready.

I watched her disappear behind the door, feeling strangely conflicted. I definitely needed to find a job and pay her back as soon as possible, but...

Walking around campus with a cute girl?

Yeah, I wasn’t about to say no to that.

Just as I was spacing out, Natsuko-san popped her head out of her room. “Taro-kun, what about breakfast?”

“Oh, right! Actually, before that—” I leaned against her doorframe and lowered my voice. “I got a question.”

“Hmm?”

“Why the hell is a yakuza like Nike allowed to live here?”

Natsuko-san blinked. Then, to my absolute shock, she burst out laughing.

“What?” I frowned. “What’s so funny?”

“Oh, dear, you really think Nike-san is yakuza?” she giggled, covering her mouth. “That’s the funniest thing I’ve heard in a long time.”

“I’m serious! That guy’s ruining my chances at a job! I swear, if I find proof, I’m gonna expose him and get him kicked out of here!”

Her expression softened. “Taro-kun. I can assure you, Nike-san is not a yakuza. He may be a little rough around the edges, but he’s a good man.”

“Are you saying this because he threatened you to keep quiet?” I squinted. “Blink twice if you need help.”

“Oh my, you’re a funny one.” She smiled and shook her head. “But truly, you’re misunderstanding something big.”

Yeah, sure. I’d get to the bottom of this eventually. For now, I let it slide and prepared for my trip to campus.

＊　＊　＊

The train ride was uneventful—except for the part where O-Yuki-chan nearly got trapped in the ticket gate.

"O-Yuki-chan! You have to go through faster!"

"Ah! I thought it would open longer!"

I sighed, pulling her through before the gate could shut on her again.

"I don’t understand," she muttered, staring at the machine like it had personally offended her. "Why is it so aggressive?"

"It’s not aggressive. It’s just… efficient."

She looked thoroughly unconvinced.

We made it to Musashi University without further incident. The campus was huge—way bigger than I’d imagined. Wide open spaces, modern buildings, students bustling around. A fresh breeze rustled the trees, and the distant chatter of students gave the place a lively atmosphere.

"Wow…" O-Yuki-chan whispered beside me, eyes wide with awe.

"First time seeing a university?"

She nodded slowly. "I’ve read about them, of course. But being here feels different. It’s so… grand. And open."

"Yeah, it’s a pretty nice campus."

She took a deep breath, as if trying to soak in the atmosphere. "I can’t believe I’m really going to study here…"

I glanced at her. "You… really wanted this, huh?"

Her fingers tightened around the strap of her bag. "Yes. More than anything."

Something about the way she said it made me pause. There was more to it than just excitement—something deeper. Something she wasn’t saying.

"You know," I started, "I’ve been meaning to ask—why weren’t you allowed to come here alone?"

She blinked, startled. "Oh, that…"

For a moment, she hesitated. Then, she smiled, but it didn’t quite reach her eyes. "They just worry too much. They think I’ll get lost, or something bad will happen."

That sounded like an excuse. A weak one, at that.

But before I could press further, she grabbed my arm. "Let’s go look around!"

We explored the campus together, checking out the different buildings. The lecture halls, the library, the cafeteria.

"So this is where students eat?" O-Yuki-chan asked, peering inside.

"Yeah. It’s not bad, actually. Some places even have all-you-can-eat options."

Her eyes sparkled. "That sounds amazing!"

I chuckled. "Yeah, but you have to pace yourself. Freshmen always overeat and regret it later."

She nodded seriously, like I had just imparted great wisdom upon her.

As we walked past a bulletin board, she suddenly stopped.

"Look! Clubs!"

I followed her gaze. The board was covered in flyers—music clubs, sports teams, cultural groups.

"Are you planning to join one?" I asked.

"I’d love to! I want to experience everything this world has to offer!"

This world? I frowned, but she had already moved on, chattering excitedly about which clubs looked interesting.

We spent the next couple of hours wandering the campus, watching students chat and laugh as they passed by. For a moment, I forgot all my problems. Forgot about my missing money, my job search, the strange atmosphere of the dorm.

For the first time since arriving in Tokyo, things felt… normal.

By the time we headed back, the sun was already starting to set. The golden light cast long shadows over the quiet streets, and for once, I didn’t feel like a complete failure.

Sure, I still had no job. No money. No idea how I was going to survive.

But today had been fun.

O-Yuki-chan had fun too. And for some reason, that made me feel like things would be okay.

That feeling lasted right up until we reached the dorm.

Because the moment we turned the corner, a deep, earth-shaking *roar* echoed through the air.

I froze.

O-Yuki-chan gasped.

And then I saw it—looming over Sakura Saku Dorm, wings outstretched against the twilight sky.

A massive, unmistakable silhouette.

Like—it's like—a dragon?
